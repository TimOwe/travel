# General info
- Google maps is fairly useless in Korea - use Naver or Kakao maps - This is better for nav but also for finding restaurants near you
- Seoul is chaotic: Seoul on weekends is absolute chaos, try to avoid major shopping areas or attractions on these days. Additionally, avoid around Gwanghwamun gate at the main palace on weekends, there are lots of protests every week and it’s miserable to be around. 
- You can often beat the crowd at lunch places by eating lunch early, like 11:30. Similarly, we were able to walk into a michelin bib gourmand spot which usually has huge lines by going at 8pm on a monday, with the kitchen closing at 8:15 for a 9pm close - it kind of pains me to recommend you going to eat somewhere just before the kitchen closes, but eating outside of regular meal hours gets you access to busy places with less crowds. 

# ESIM/Data
 You can buy a cheaper e-sim from several places but all the big korean telecom companies offer esims with unlimited data and phone numbers, which is really useful for virtual queues for food and comes in handy often (i.e signing up for an app and you need a domestic phone number for sms verification). Note that if you buy a provider from a HK based provider, even when roaming outside of hong kong, you won’t be able to load Tik-Tok since it is banned there. Save yourself some headaches, spend a little more and get a local company’s e-sim:

https://roaming.kt.com/esim/eng/main.asp

# Transport
Use Naver maps instead of google maps -- it is okay now, but local businesses will only keep their information up to date on Korean apps (and often won’t be on google at all), so info on google maps for a lot of stores is either missing key info like opening hours or is out of date. You might find it useful to save everything on google maps when planning, but then on the day in Korea, pre-plan a route with Naver to double check everything is where you expect

Transport card - You need a T-money card for busses and trains. You can get it at any of the convenience stores by just asking for t-money, and you can re-load it at any of the train stations as needed

Uber works in Korea - the Kakao T app is also good. I would avoid the specific foreigner friendly taxi app they made, as it is not very good. Generally taxis are very cheap, and in a group it is a great option over public transport outside of rush hour

From the airport, catch the bus into the city; Incheon is really far from the city and calling an uber/taxi will be expensive. Additionally, if you opt to catch the train in from the airport, there are likely to be really long transfers which is painful with luggage. The airport transfer bus network is quite large. Naver maps will likely suggest this route, and you can use that to plan. At the airport, look for the machines and buy a ticket using the stop name naver tells you. When returning back to the airport, you can pay cash or with T-money. You get one piece of checked luggage stored in the bottom for free

# Shopping
## Clothing
Tip: Anything imported is usually pretty expensive; even if it’s from Japan, like you will seriously see Japanese stuff cheaper in Sydney than you will in Seoul in most cases. If you shop domestic products, I think due to lax labour laws (and proximity to China), everything can be much cheaper than you may expect. 

You can get tax free almost everywhere on purchases over something ike 15,000 won (~$16). Some places will ask for your passport and do it on the spot, other places will just give you a receipt, and at the airport you will need find the tax free stall to get your refund. Carry your passport just in case
